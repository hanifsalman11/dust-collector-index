Frequently Asked Questions (Jet Dust Collector Filter Upgrade)

How many actual CFM do I need for a 4.0-inch dust port on Jet Dust Collector Filter Upgrade?

A 4.0-inch duct has an internal cross-sectional area of 0.087 sq. ft. Multiplying by 4,000 FPM gives 350 actual working CFM. Because manufacturer 'Free-Air' ratings ignore duct and filter drag, choose a 1.5 HP collector advertised at 580+ CFM.

Why does a 6-inch duct move more than double the air of a 4-inch duct?

By circle geometry ($A = \pi r^2$), a 4-inch pipe has an area of 0.087 sq. ft. (maxing out at ~350 CFM at 4,000 FPM), whereas a 6-inch pipe has an area of 0.196 sq. ft. (2.25x larger), allowing 785 CFM to flow at 4,000 FPM with less than half the friction loss.

How much static pressure loss does a cyclone separator and flex hose add?

A typical cyclone lid or 2-stage separator adds 1.8" to 2.5" WG of static pressure loss, a clean 1-micron canister filter adds ~1.2" WG, and every 5 feet of 4-inch flex hose adds ~1.1" WG—totaling 5.2" WG of system resistance.
